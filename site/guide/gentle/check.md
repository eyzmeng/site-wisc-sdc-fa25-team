## Part 0: Know your system

Computers can be roughly divided into three camps: Windows, Mac OS,
and the Unix rebels (most notably Linux, and most popularly Canonical's
Ubuntu Linux<!-- I wonder if this is still true? -->).  **Windows**
(Windows NT) picked up from MS-DOS, and naturally inherited many
things from it like the CRLF line endings and **cmd.exe**.  Mac OS
was originally its own unique thing (so unique, in fact, that it was
often given the retronym "*Classic* Mac OS"), but joined the Unix camp
after when it was renamed to OS X at the turn of the millenium,
which sometime later got rebranded as **macOS** again, this time
without the space and without the leading capital letter.

Then there is Linux, which was an effort to re-make Minix, but
without Minix (and also *free* like stuff made by the GNU project).
Linux inherits the Unix lineage too, though it doesn't always claim
to stick around much.  (After all, GNU is literally a recursive
acronym for GNU's Not Unix... (but I think that came more from the
fact that GNU doesn't want to become proprietary like UNIX, with
trademark and everything involved.)  But anyways, even though Linux
is not the SUS-compliant kind of Unix, it still follows Unix in
many respects, enough that you could *pretend* it is a Unix
(The same goes for macOS except I guess macOS is in fact a legit
SUS-complaint UNIX certified by Open Group?  It's not what people
*usually* think of, the command-line interface is niche but nice,
and the fact that Apple even bothers to pay Open Group for the
certification fee certainly says something.))

So ancestorally, (post-OS X) Mac OS (hereinafter **macOS**) and
Linux are most directly related.  Windows is the odd one out
here, relatively speaking.  Though in the light of various
kinds of Unix-like operating systems (where even Linux itself
has a wide variety distribution between say, Ubuntu and Alpine)
you may start to feel that Windows is *the* oddball.

If you use operating systems other than Windows, macOS, and
Linux, you should let me know as I would most likely know
next to nothing about them.  (I've used a bit of FreeBSD and
NetBSD, but I know very little about those...)
